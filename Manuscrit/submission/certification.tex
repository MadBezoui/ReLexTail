\section{Decision-focused certification}
\label{sec:certrelex}

Section~\ref{sec:compute} certifies with a condition that asks more than the
decision needs. Theorem~\ref{thm:categorystability} requires \emph{every}
candidate to keep \emph{every} risk coordinate inside its cell, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:certsound} is applied through enclosures in which the
shared normalisation anchors are widened independently for each candidate. This
section replaces both with conditions matched to the question actually asked --
does the recommendation survive? -- proves that neither replacement weakens
soundness, and measures what the change buys. The declared rule is untouched:
on fixed inputs the engine of this section and the engine of
Section~\ref{sec:compute} select the same alternative. What differs is what can
be proved about that alternative and at what cost.

\subsection{The decision region and its radius}
Fix $A$, a retained multiset $\Q$, a resolution vector $\delta$ and the grid
convention of Section~\ref{sec:relextail}. For a nominal winner $x_0$ that is
unique, the \emph{decision region} is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_{x_0}=\bigl\{b:\Psi(x_0;b)<_{\mathrm{lex}}\Psi(y;b)\ \text{for every }y\neq x_0\bigr\},
\label{eq:decisionregion}
\end{equation}
and for the nested boxes $\mathcal B(p)$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:box} the
\emph{decision radius} is
$\rho^\star(x_0)=\sup\{p:\mathcal B(p)\subseteq\mathcal R_{x_0}\}$.
The computational object is a certified inner region for
$\mathcal R_{x_0}$, reported together with the leaves that were not resolved.
Three conventions matter and are not negotiable in what follows. A qualifying
set that turns out to be empty is reported as ``no unique-winner certificate'',
never converted into positive stability. A tied nominal optimum requires a
separately defined set-preservation contract and is recorded and skipped rather
than resolved by tie-breaking. And the procedure is one-sided: failure to
certify at level $p$ means only that the budget did not suffice, so an upper
bound on $\rho^\star$ can come only from a verified counterexample, never from
a failed search.

\subsection{Certifying the decision, not every coordinate}
\begin{proposition}[Decision-focused soundness]
\label{prop:decisionfocus}
Let $\beta$ be a box on which the retained multiset is fixed, and let
$D^L(\cdot;\beta)\le D(\cdot;b)\le D^U(\cdot;\beta)$ coordinatewise for every
$b\in\beta$. If
\[
\Psi\bigl(D^U(x_0;\beta)\bigr)<_{\mathrm{lex}}\Psi\bigl(D^L(y;\beta)\bigr)
\qquad\text{for every }y\neq x_0,
\]
then $\beta\subseteq\mathcal R_{x_0}$. No candidate is required to retain any
category, and no coordinate of $\Psi$ beyond the first differing one is
examined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every coordinate of $\Psi$ is a nondecreasing functional of $D$
coordinatewise: order statistics, the maximum, the upper-tail means of
Equation~\eqref{eq:tail} and the category map $B_\delta$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:category} all are. Hence for $b\in\beta$,
$\Psi(D(x_0;b))\le\Psi(D^U(x_0;\beta))$ and
$\Psi(D^L(y;\beta))\le\Psi(D(y;b))$ componentwise, and componentwise
inequality implies $\le_{\mathrm{lex}}$. The lexicographic order is total and
transitive, so
$\Psi(x_0;b)\le_{\mathrm{lex}}\Psi(D^U(x_0;\beta))<_{\mathrm{lex}}
\Psi(D^L(y;\beta))\le_{\mathrm{lex}}\Psi(y;b)$ for every $y\ne x_0$ and every
$b\in\beta$.
\end{proof}

The chain uses no retention of categories anywhere. Category retention is
therefore sufficient but not necessary, and the gap is not a technicality.

\begin{proposition}[The global condition can be vacuous while the decision is certain]
\label{prop:vacuous}
There exist a candidate set with rational entries, a resolution vector and a
level $p>0$ such that every candidate changes at least one categorical
coordinate between the lower and the upper enclosure over
$\mathcal B(p)$ -- so $\mu_{\mathrm{cell}}$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:categorystability} certifies nothing -- while the test of
Proposition~\ref{prop:decisionfocus} certifies the unique winner against every
rival on the whole of $\mathcal B(p)$ without any subdivision.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By exhibition. Example~E-A of the reproducibility archive is a
$5\times4$ matrix whose entries are integer multiples of $1/64$, hence exact in
binary double precision. At $p=0.01$ all \crxExampleALeaving{} candidates leave
at least one cell, so the all-candidate condition refuses the root box; the
pairwise test of Proposition~\ref{prop:decisionfocus} succeeds against all four
rivals at that same root box. Both facts are recomputed by
\texttt{certrelex.examples.check\_boundary}.
\end{proof}

The mechanism is the one anticipated in Section~\ref{sec:theory}: a coordinate
may be free to move because it belongs to an alternative that is not in
contention, because it sits on a boundary that is structurally invariant, or
because it occurs after a comparison that is already decided. None of those
motions can change the recommendation, and a condition that forbids them
measures the wrong thing.

\subsection{Preserving the shared normalisation anchors}
Write $v_x(b)=q(r(x;b))$ for a retained aggregate probe. All candidates share
the same two anchors $q^\star(b)$ and $q^w(b)$ at a given $b$, and
Equation~\eqref{eq:disap} reads $D_q(x;b)=g\bigl(v_x(b),q^\star(b),q^w(b)\bigr)$
with
\begin{equation}
g(v,a,c)=\frac{v-a}{c-a}.
\label{eq:sharedquotient}
\end{equation}
Widening $v-a$ and $c-a$ into two independent intervals and dividing them lets
the numerator take an extreme value against a denominator that the same $a$
could not have produced. Keeping $a$ inside a single expression removes that.

\begin{proposition}[Shared-anchor enclosure]
\label{prop:sharedanchor}
Let $v\in[v^L,v^U]$, $a\in[a^L,a^U]$, $c\in[c^L,c^U]$ with $c^L>a^U$. Then
$g$ is strictly increasing in $v$ and, for each fixed $v$, monotone in $a$ and
in $c$ separately, with
\[
\frac{\partial g}{\partial c}=-\frac{v-a}{(c-a)^2},\qquad
\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}=\frac{v-c}{(c-a)^2},
\]
each of constant sign in the variable being varied. Consequently the range of
$g$ over the product box is attained at the four corners of
$[a^L,a^U]\times[c^L,c^U]$, evaluated at $v=v^L$ for the minimum and $v=v^U$
for the maximum. The resulting interval is contained in the independent
quotient $[v^L-a^U,\ v^U-a^L]/[c^L-a^U,\ c^U-a^L]$, and the containment is
strict whenever $a^L<a^U$ and $v^L\ge a^U$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two derivatives are as displayed. For fixed $v$ the sign of
$\partial g/\partial c$ is that of $-(v-a)$, which does not depend on $c$; the
sign of $\partial g/\partial a$ is that of $v-c$, which does not depend on $a$.
A function that is monotone in each variable separately on a rectangle attains
its extrema at the rectangle's corners: minimise first over $c$ at an endpoint,
then over $a$ at an endpoint. Monotonicity in $v$ places the minimum at $v^L$
and the maximum at $v^U$. For containment, note that each corner value is one
of the four quotients $(v-a)/(c-a)$ with $a\in\{a^L,a^U\}$ and
$c\in\{c^L,c^U\}$, and every such quotient lies in the independent interval
because $v-a$ lies in $[v^L-a^U,v^U-a^L]$ and $c-a$ in $[c^L-a^U,c^U-a^L]$. If
$v^L\ge a^U$ the corner minimum is $(v^L-a^U)/(c^U-a^U)$, whereas the
independent lower endpoint is $(v^L-a^U)/(c^U-a^L)$; the denominators differ by
$a^U-a^L>0$ with a nonnegative numerator, so the inequality is strict, and
symmetrically at the upper end.
\end{proof}

Two further identities are used and are not claimed as new. Singleton
disappointments are bound-invariant by Equation~\eqref{eq:singleton}, so
$m$ of the at most $m+2$ canonical coordinates carry no width however large the
box is; the verifier of Section~\ref{sec:certrelex-replay} rechecks that
invariance at the box corners rather than assuming it. And branching is
directed at the criteria whose normalised enclosure is widest among the
candidates still involved in an unresolved comparison, because those are the
only bound coordinates that can still move the decision; this changes where
effort goes and not what is provable.

\begin{proposition}[Cancellation-induced slack is not asymptotic]
\label{prop:slack}
There exist a rational candidate set and a level $p>0$ at which the enclosure
of Proposition~\ref{prop:sharedanchor} certifies the unique winner on the whole
of $\mathcal B(p)$ in a single box, while the independent quotient -- identical
in every other respect -- leaves part of the box uncertified after a budget
three orders of magnitude larger.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By exhibition. Example~E-B is a $4\times3$ matrix over the denominator $64$.
At $p=0.05$ the joint enclosure resolves the root box in
\crxExampleBJointBoxes{} box; the independent enclosure has certified only
\crxExampleBIndepVolume\% of the volume after \crxExampleBIndepBoxes{}
processed boxes. The two runs differ only in
Equation~\eqref{eq:sharedquotient}. Recomputed by
\texttt{certrelex.examples.check\_slack}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Anytime brackets, refusal and independent replay}
\label{sec:certrelex-replay}
Certified leaves are never revisited and unresolved leaves always retain their
volume, so an interrupted run returns a valid certified region together with a
valid bound on the uncertified volume, and continuing can only move volume from
the second into the first. A box is \emph{refused} outright when a normalising
denominator or a probe active range is not certifiably positive on it; refusal
leaves the box unresolved and never becomes positive stability.

Because the search is one-sided, an upper bound on $\rho^\star$ must come from
elsewhere. We obtain it from an exact rational oracle that evaluates the
declared rule at a single bound vector with no floating-point step: a $b$ at
which the oracle names a different unique winner is a verified counterexample
and bounds $\rho^\star$ from above. Example~E-C is a $4\times3$ rational
instance whose nominal winner is candidate~\crxExampleCNominal{}; inside
$\mathcal B(\crxExampleCUpper)$ there is a bound vector at which the oracle
names candidate~\crxExampleCWitness{} uniquely, while the engine certifies
$\rho^\star\ge\crxExampleCLower$. The pair
$[\crxExampleCLower,\ \crxExampleCUpper]$ is a genuine two-sided bracket, which
is what an honest anytime certificate should return in place of a single
number.

Every certificate is replayed by a verifier written to be simpler than the
search and independent of it: it imports nothing from the interval or
enclosure modules, works entirely in exact rational arithmetic, and knows
nothing about gates, budgets or branching. It is handed a leaf and a claimed
winner and recomputes the enclosure and the comparison. A leaf both routes
accept has been certified twice, by different arithmetic. The full numerical
contract -- reference rule, operational selector, certification arithmetic and
verifier, and what each does \emph{not} claim -- is recorded in the archive.

\subsection{Fixed-budget certification experiments}
\label{sec:certrelex-experiments}
The study is a fixed-rule comparison: the same declared rule, probes,
uncertainty box, arithmetic contract, box budget and hardware in every arm, so
that any difference is attributable to the certification machinery and not to a
different recommendation. Instances come from six development families and two
locked stress families (near-tied margins; correlated heavy-tailed measurement
noise) at $m\in\{3,6,10\}$ with eight replicates each,
\crxInstances{} candidate sets in all, certified on a shortlist of
\crxShortlist{} alternatives. The split is a deterministic function of the
replicate index and was fixed in source before any run; the 90 instances of the
earlier sections remain the development baseline and are not used as
confirmatory evidence. Resolution is $\delta=\crxResolution$ throughout and the
focal level is $p=\crxFocalLevel$. Each arm adds exactly one component to the
one above:

The primary endpoint is the certified lower bound $\rho^{\mathrm{lo}}$ on the
decision radius, obtained by bisection at the matched budget; it is continuous,
paired within instance, and one value per arm. The two prespecified contrasts
are P1 $=$ A1 $-$ A0, isolating the decision focus, and P2 $=$ A2 $-$ A1,
isolating dependency preservation, both on the locked test split with Holm
correction inside that family. Every other contrast is labelled exploratory.
The independent unit is the instance. Because the study was not preregistered
with a third party, $p$-values are descriptive.

\paragraph{Correctness (E0).}
Across \crxCorrectnessInstances{} instances, \crxOracleDraws{} bound vectors
were drawn from inside certified leaves and evaluated by the exact rational
oracle, and \crxReplayLeaves{} certified leaves were replayed by the
independent verifier. There were \crxOracleViolations{} falsified certificates
and \crxReplayFailures{} replay failures. Testing supplements
Propositions~\ref{prop:decisionfocus} and~\ref{prop:sharedanchor}; it does not
substitute for them, and a zero count is evidence of the absence of the errors
searched for, not of soundness in general.

\paragraph{Mechanism (E1).}
At the root box the widest disappointment enclosure averages
\crxWidthIndep{} under the independent quotient and \crxWidthJoint{} under the
shared-anchor quotient. The coordinate that decides the nominal comparison is
categorical on \crxDecisiveCategorical{} of the \crxDecisiveTotal{} instances
with a resolvable root box; on those, the median enclosure width of that
coordinate falls from \crxDecisiveWidthIndep{} categories to
\crxDecisiveWidthJoint. The remaining instances are decided on a tail mean and
are excluded from that statistic because the two are not in the same units. The
share of instances certified at the root box with no subdivision at all rises
from \crxRootCertifiedIndep\% to \crxRootCertifiedJoint\%.

\paragraph{What this does not show.}
These are certification results and nothing more. They do not show that
ReLexTail chooses better than another method, that its recommendations carry
lower external regret, or that any calibration guarantee holds; the
corresponding tracks were not run, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} records
them as unestablished. The instances are generated, the shortlist size is
declared rather than elicited, and the stress families test extrapolation
without licensing any claim about arbitrary deployment shift. A radius reported
as zero always means ``not certified within this budget''.
