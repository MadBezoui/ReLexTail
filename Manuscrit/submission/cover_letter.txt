Dear Editors,

Please consider “Resolution-Aware Lexicographic Tail-Regret Selection under Uncertain Normalisation Bounds” as a Research Article in the Journal of Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis.

The manuscript studies a decision-aiding rule for selecting from a fixed multicriteria candidate set when the reporting resolution of declared concerns matters. ReLexTail combines categories of maximum and upper-tail probe disappointment with exact lexicographic refinement. It establishes finite-set properties, gives a sound interval-elimination condition under normalisation uncertainty, and describes a sequential formulation for continuous polyhedra.

Its main new result concerns what can be certified about a recommendation when the normalisation bounds are uncertain. The natural sufficient condition asks every candidate to keep every risk coordinate inside its resolution cell, and it fails whenever any coordinate touches a boundary, including coordinates that cannot affect the outcome. We prove that a recommendation can be certified throughout a bound box with no candidate retaining any category, and that keeping a probe's shared normalisation anchors inside a single quotient yields an enclosure contained in the independent one. Two small rational examples show that neither gap is asymptotic: on one, the all-candidate condition certifies nothing while the decision is certain over the whole box; on the other, anchor slack alone decides whether the box is certified. On a separate benchmark whose splits were fixed before the run, the two changes raise the certified decision radius at a matched budget, and an exact rational oracle and an independent replay verifier falsified none of the emitted certificates.

The paper connects ordered aggregation and robustness analysis with an explicit, replayable recommendation. Its 90-instance synthetic experiment uses 500 matched bound perturbations per level and held-out preference vectors, reports paired uncertainty summaries, and includes grid-origin and representation ablations. A public building-simulation dataset provides an application-oriented reproducibility check, while a constructed supplier example demonstrates the decisive category comparison. The manuscript distinguishes theoretical guarantees, simulation evidence and unresolved interval uncertainty, and does not claim validation on an operational decision. A claim-to-evidence table states for every claim whether it is proved, measured, or unestablished; decision quality against external preference populations, any calibration guarantee, and priority over the closest literature are listed as unestablished rather than implied.

The submission package contains the manuscript, separate vector figures, full captions, reproducible scripts and generated data. I believe its focus on preference modelling, interpretation and qualified robustness is relevant to the journal's decision-analysis readership.
